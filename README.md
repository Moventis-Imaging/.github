## About us

Some patients don't get better after knee replacement surgery, and standard imaging can't tell you why.

Moventis Imaging is developing a new approach to painful or unclear knee replacement follow-up. With a **CT-compatible loading setup** and **structured reporting**, we aim to give orthopaedic and radiology teams an additional mechanical view of how an implant behaves under load.

Our work builds on research experience in **dynamic musculoskeletal imaging**, **motion analysis** and **orthopaedic workflow development**. This organisation is where we share code and tools from that work.

## How it works

A loading board reproduces the compressive conditions of weight-bearing while the patient lies supine. It works with the standard clinical CT scanner already in the department, so no extra scanner or scanner modification is needed.

1. **Existing CT scanner** is used as is
2. **Moventis device** is positioned on the CT table
3. **Quick alignment**, no changes to the scanner
4. **Unloaded scan** of the patient's knee
5. **Loaded scan** with controlled axial load applied
6. **Automated analysis, visualisation and reporting** comparing both scans

The result is a set of structured visual and numerical outputs that teams can review and discuss alongside standard care.

## Who it's for

| Audience | Use case |
|---|---|
| 🦴 **Orthopaedic surgeons** | Complicated or persistently painful knee cases, e.g. after ACL tears, osteotomies or kinematic alignment |
| 🩻 **Radiology teams** | A paired acquisition protocol that fits existing CT workflows |
| ⚙️ **Implant manufacturers** | Preclinical assessment of implant behaviour and migration under load |

> [!NOTE]
> Moventis Imaging is in development. It is **not a certified medical device** and is **not intended for diagnosis**. Measured movement between unloaded and loaded conditions does not by itself indicate loosening or explain symptoms. Outputs are meant to support interpretation alongside standard care, not to replace it.
